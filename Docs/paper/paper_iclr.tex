\title{Vmem-$\varphi$: Zero-Cost Out-of-Distribution Detection in Spiking\\
Neural Networks from Membrane-Potential Statistics}

\begin{abstract}
Spiking neural networks (SNNs) on event cameras are deployed precisely where the
standard out-of-distribution (OOD) toolkit cannot run: on neuromorphic silicon with no
floating-point logits, no gradients, and no budget for a second forward pass. We observe
that the sub-threshold membrane potential $V_{\mathrm{mem}}(t)$ of the Parametric
Leaky-Integrate-and-Fire (PLIF) neurons is a \emph{free byproduct} of the forward pass --
a register the substrate already maintains -- and that its per-channel statistics
$\varphi=[\mu,\sigma^2,\kappa]$ (a $2112$-D descriptor over $704$ channels $\times$ $4$
layers) form an OOD signal at \emph{zero added inference compute} -- a register read plus three
moments, with no second forward pass -- on which a lightweight host-side detector then operates.
We contribute
\textbf{Gen1-C}, a reproducible, seeded event-camera corruption benchmark of six
sensor-grounded corruptions $\times$ five severities -- each traced to a concrete hardware
failure mechanism -- plus a released membrane-statistics feature set so OOD detectors can
be compared without re-running SNN inference. We give a closed-form leaky-integrator theory
that predicts, per corruption, which membrane moment moves and therefore which corruptions
are detectable and which are \emph{provably} not, and we confirm with a motivation
experiment that feeding these corruptions to a pretrained state-of-the-art hybrid SNN--ANN
detector (no retraining) collapses its mAP. Finally we introduce the
Manifold-Decomposition Detector (MDD), which decomposes each frame's deviation into
orthogonal manifold axes scored two-sided, taking the contraction corruptions above chance
for the first time; one unsupervised, corruption-blind score reaches $\geq 0.85$ AUROC on
four of six corruptions with a short, bounded-latency $64$-frame window (not whole-recording
pooling). We report honest negatives --
\texttt{polarity\_flip} (a network invariance) and \texttt{spatial\_dropout} (a signature
global pooling discards) -- and frame them as an open challenge: the Achilles' heel of
event-based perception.
\advisor{framing agreed -- dataset/benchmark as a contribution; report hard corruptions as a discovered open challenge.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Event cameras report per-pixel brightness changes asynchronously at microsecond resolution,
high dynamic range, and low power; paired with spiking neural networks (SNNs) they form the
canonical low-power perception stack for neuromorphic hardware. As these systems move toward
safety-critical deployment (automotive, robotics), a basic question becomes unavoidable:
\emph{when the sensor input drifts away from the training distribution, can the system
notice?} Out-of-distribution (OOD) detection is the standard answer for artificial neural
networks (ANNs), but essentially every method in that literature assumes a capability the
neuromorphic execution model does not provide -- floating-point logits (MSP, Energy),
input gradients (ODIN, GradNorm), or a second forward pass over the features (ReAct,
ANN-feature Mahalanobis/kNN). On a chip that emits spikes and integer events under a strict
energy budget, these are not merely expensive but architecturally infeasible
(Section~\ref{sec:hardware}).

We start from a signal that is free exactly where the alternatives are impossible. The
Parametric Leaky-Integrate-and-Fire (PLIF) neuron maintains a sub-threshold membrane
potential $V_{\mathrm{mem}}(t)$ -- physical state the substrate already updates on every
time step to decide whether to spike. Reading it and reducing it to a few per-channel
moments adds zero multiply--accumulates and zero weight fetches. We call the resulting
$2112$-dimensional descriptor $\varphi$ and ask how far this free signal goes as an OOD
detector against realistic event-camera corruptions, each grounded in a concrete sensor
failure mechanism (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}).

\paragraph{Contributions.} We make three key contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A zero-cost membrane OOD signal and the Gen1-C benchmark.} We introduce
        $\varphi$, an OOD signal harvested from the PLIF membrane at \emph{zero additional
        compute}, and argue it is the richest OOD signal available at zero added inference cost
        on the target neuromorphic hardware -- needing no logits, gradients, or second forward
        pass, and provably subsuming spike-rate signals (Section~\ref{sec:hardware}). To evaluate it we
        release \textbf{Gen1-C}: a reproducible (seeded, deterministic, model-agnostic)
        event-camera corruption benchmark of six corruptions $\times$ five monotonic
        severities, each mapped to a physical hardware failure mode
        (Section~\ref{sec:corruptions}, Figure~\ref{fig:schematic}), in the
        ImageNet-C~\citep{hendrycks2019imagenetc} tradition, plus a released
        membrane-statistics feature set.
  \item \textbf{A closed-form theory of detectability, empirically motivated.} We derive,
        from the PLIF state equation and with no parameters fit to corruption labels, a
        leaky-integrator theory that predicts which membrane moment each corruption moves
        and therefore which corruptions are detectable -- a dilation/contraction/invariant
        taxonomy (Section~\ref{sec:theory}). We further prove the membrane mean, variance, and
        the Mahalanobis OOD score all increase \emph{monotonically} with corruption severity
        (Theorems~\ref{thm:drift}--\ref{thm:score}), and that the sub-threshold potential
        \emph{strictly subsumes} spike counts for OOD (Proposition~\ref{prop:spike}). We confirm
        the stakes with a motivation
        experiment: a pretrained SOTA hybrid SNN--ANN detector~\citep{neftci2025hybrid}
        loses substantial mAP under these corruptions with no retraining
        (Section~\ref{sec:motivation}).
  \item \textbf{The Manifold-Decomposition Detector (MDD), and an open challenge.} Following
        the geometric diagnosis, MDD stops collapsing $\varphi$ to one scalar and instead
        scores orthogonal, two-sided manifold axes fused by a calibrated OR
        (Section~\ref{sec:mdd}); it takes the contraction corruptions above chance for the
        first time and reaches $\geq 0.85$ AUROC on four of six corruptions with a bounded
        $64$-frame window (Section~\ref{sec:window}); whole-recording pooling only sharpens the two
        easiest and we do not lean on it. We report \texttt{polarity\_flip} and \texttt{spatial\_dropout} as
        honest, theory-predicted residuals and release them as a community challenge
        (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{OOD detection for ANNs.}
The dominant families score a post-hoc statistic of a trained network: maximum softmax
probability~\citep{hendrycks2017msp}, temperature-scaled and input-perturbed
logits~\citep{liang2018odin}, free energy~\citep{liu2020energy}, rectified
activations~\citep{sun2021react}, virtual-logit matching~\citep{wang2022vim}, gradient
norms~\citep{huang2021gradnorm}, and distance/density on penultimate features
(Mahalanobis~\citep{lee2018mahalanobis}, deep $k$-NN~\citep{sun2022knn}). All presuppose
floating-point readouts, gradients, or repeated forward passes -- capabilities absent from
the spiking, feed-forward, integer-event execution model of neuromorphic hardware
(Section~\ref{sec:hardware} makes this precise).

\paragraph{OOD detection for SNNs (and how we differ).}
\advisor{point 1 -- confirm the task is new. Honest answer: OOD-for-SNN exists; OUR task framing is new.}
OOD detection \emph{inside} an SNN is not unprecedented. Most directly, Mart\'inez et al.~\citep{martinez2023snnood}
characterise the internal activations of hidden layers via \emph{spike-count} patterns and add
an explainability map, evaluated on image (including event-based) \emph{classification}
benchmarks. Our work is distinct on four axes that, in combination, define a new task:
(i)~we read the \emph{sub-threshold membrane potential}, not spike counts -- a continuous,
pre-threshold signal we prove \emph{strictly subsumes} the binarised spikes for OOD
(Proposition~\ref{prop:spike}: spike counts are blind to sub-threshold shift);
(ii)~the host model is an event-camera \emph{object detector} (Prophesee Gen1), not a
classifier, so there is no class posterior to lean on; (iii)~the distribution shifts are
\emph{event-stream corruptions} we define and release as a benchmark, not held-out classes;
and (iv)~we make the \emph{zero-compute / neuromorphic-register} feasibility argument central.
We therefore position the paper as introducing the \emph{event-camera membrane-statistics OOD}
task and the Gen1-C benchmark, while explicitly crediting prior SNN-OOD work.

\paragraph{Event-camera robustness and corruption benchmarks.}
Robustness benchmarks built from synthetic corruptions are standard for frame cameras
(ImageNet-C~\citep{hendrycks2019imagenetc}); the event-camera analogue is comparatively
underdeveloped. Gen1-C (Section~\ref{sec:corruptions}) fills this gap with corruptions
grounded in concrete sensor failure modes (Figure~\ref{fig:schematic},
Table~\ref{tab:corr-taxonomy}), seeded for exact reproducibility, and operating on the
standard histogram tensor so they compose with any event model.

\paragraph{The host detector.}
Our membrane signal is read from the pretrained hybrid SNN--ANN detector of
Neftci et al.~\citep{neftci2025hybrid}, a strong Gen1 baseline that fuses a spiking front-end (SpikingJelly
PLIF~\citep{fang2021plif}) with an ANN detection head. We treat it as read-only and never
retrain it -- both for the OOD signal and for the motivation experiment of
Section~\ref{sec:motivation}.

\paragraph{Membrane potential as a hardware register.}
On Loihi~\citep{davies2018loihi}, BrainScaleS~\citep{schemmel2010brainscales}, and
SpiNNaker~\citep{furber2014spinnaker}, $V_{\mathrm{mem}}$ is state the neuron already
maintains; harvesting $\varphi$ from it is an $O(\text{channels})$ read. This is the basis of
the feasibility argument in Section~\ref{sec:hardware}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We present (i) the zero-cost membrane descriptor $\varphi$, (ii) the Gen1-C corruption suite
with its physical-mechanism grounding and algorithms, (iii) a leaky-integrator theory that
predicts per-corruption detectability, (iv) the neuromorphic-hardware feasibility argument,
and (v) the Manifold-Decomposition Detector that follows from the theory.

\subsection{The zero-cost membrane descriptor \texorpdfstring{$\varphi$}{phi}}
\label{sec:phi}

The membrane signal is read from the pretrained hybrid SNN--ANN detector with a forward hook
on all four PLIF layers ($704$ channels total). Concretely, $V$ is the neuron's membrane state
\texttt{module.v} at each of the $T{=}10$ unrolled steps (reset-inclusive: we read the raw
maintained register, we do \emph{not} excise the steps around a spike); the linear sub-threshold
analysis of Section~\ref{sec:theory} is therefore an approximation whose reset effects enter as a
bounded error term (Theorem~\ref{thm:drift}), exact in the low-firing regime. For each channel $c$
we apply Global Average Pooling (GAP) over the spatial dimensions of $V$ and compute its first
three central moments across the $T$ steps,
%
\begin{equation}
  \varphi_c = \big[\mu_c,\sigma_c^2,\kappa_c\big],\qquad
  \mu_c=\mathbb{E}[V_c],\quad \sigma_c^2=\mathrm{Var}[V_c],\quad
  \kappa_c=\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[(V_c-\mu_c)^4]}{\sigma_c^4},
  \label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}
%
stacking to a $2112$-D descriptor $\varphi$ per frame. The mean captures sustained drive, the
variance fluctuation energy, the kurtosis burstiness. Two properties drive every later result:
(i)~GAP discards spatial layout -- $\varphi$ knows \emph{how much} each channel fired, not
\emph{where}; (ii)~$\varphi$ inherits the network's invariances. A spatial-dispersion
extension \texttt{phi\_spatial} ($1408$-D: per-channel spatial variance and participation
ratio) recovers part of the structure GAP discards. Clean $\varphi$ lies on a thin, curved,
scene-dependent manifold; detection is deciding whether a new $\varphi$ lies on it.

\subsection{The Gen1-C event-camera corruption suite}
\label{sec:corruptions}

A benchmark for OOD detection is only as meaningful as the distribution shifts it tests
against. We define a controlled, reproducible battery of six corruptions that model the
dominant failure modes of event-camera front-ends, each at five monotonically increasing
severities; with the clean baseline this yields $6\times5+1=31$ runs. Each corruption perturbs
a \emph{structurally distinct} axis of the input -- DC offset, localized burst, temporal order,
global rate, polarity, or spatial support -- which is what makes the closed-form analysis of
Section~\ref{sec:theory} possible.

\paragraph{Input representation.}
Every corruption operates on the model's input tensor, so it composes with any downstream
architecture and is label-free. A sequence is a stack of $N$ event-histogram frames of shape
$(20,H,W)$, $H\times W=240\times304$ for Gen1. The $20$ channels are two polarities $\times$
ten $50\,\mu$s time bins: channels $0$--$9$ ON (oldest to newest), $10$--$19$ OFF. Counts are
\texttt{uint8}; corruptions promote to a wider type internally and clip to $[0,255]$.

\paragraph{Design principles.}
\textbf{(i) Reproducibility:} every random decision is drawn from a single seeded NumPy
generator, so a (corruption, severity, seed) triple is deterministic. \textbf{(ii) Hardware
agnosticism:} operations dispatch to NumPy or CuPy. \textbf{(iii) Severity monotonicity:}
severity $1$ is mild and $5$ severe, with monotone parameters (Table~\ref{tab:corr-severity}),
enabling the Spearman-$\rho$ analysis of Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{hot\_pixel} -- defective / stuck pixels, fixed-pattern noise.}
        Fabrication defects, bias-current mismatch and junction leakage make individual DVS
        pixels cross their change threshold with no scene change, firing continuously at fixed
        coordinates. The rate of such self-triggering rises with temperature and with
        aggressive bias settings, so it materializes as a constant DC offset at a fixed set of
        locations -- the event-camera analogue of stuck CCD/CMOS pixels.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{event\_flood} -- EMI, illumination transients, readout saturation.}
        Electromagnetic interference, LED/PWM flicker, or a sudden strong-light transient
        drives a dense burst of spurious events in a region for a short window; when the local
        event rate exceeds the readout/arbiter bandwidth the periphery saturates, so the burst
        is both localized in space and bounded in time.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{temporal\_jitter} -- clock noise, timestamp error.} Row/column
        arbitration delay, bus contention, and finite timestamp resolution misassign events to
        neighbouring time bins. The marginal per-pixel counts are essentially preserved; what
        is corrupted is the \emph{order} (and hence the temporal autocorrelation) of events.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{event\_rate\_shift} -- contrast-threshold / biasing drift, domain gap.}
        The per-pixel contrast threshold and bias currents set the event rate; temperature
        drift, re-biasing, or simply moving between a low-texture/slow-motion and a
        high-texture/fast-motion regime multiplies the global rate. We model both directions
        (under- and over-rate) with a seeded coin flip.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{polarity\_flip} -- ON/OFF misclassification.} Comparator offset and
        asymmetric ON/OFF thresholds (a documented DVS non-ideality) cause the brightness-change
        sign to be reported incorrectly for a fraction of events; under fast global illumination
        swings whole frames can have their polarity inverted.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{spatial\_dropout} -- dead-pixel clusters, occlusion.} Manufacturing dead
        regions, dust/lens contamination, condensation, or partial physical occlusion zero the
        event support over contiguous areas for the duration of the fault.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Corruption algorithms and severity schedules}
\label{sec:corr-algos}

Each corruption operates on a histogram $X\in\{0,\dots,255\}^{N\times20\times H\times W}$ and
returns a corrupted copy. \textbf{Hot pixel:} add a constant $\delta$ to every bin/frame at $n$
fixed random locations (\texttt{int16}, clipped). \textbf{Event flood:} at $b$ random
frame/patch centres add $\delta$ inside a patch covering fraction $f$ over frames within $\pm2$
of the centre. \textbf{Temporal jitter:} roll the ON block (ch.~$0$--$9$) and OFF block
(ch.~$10$--$19$) along the time-bin axis by independent integer shifts in $[-s_{\max},s_{\max}]$,
zero-filling vacated bins. \textbf{Event-rate shift:} multiply all counts by a seeded
under/over factor and clip. \textbf{Polarity flip:} swap the ON/OFF blocks on a random fraction
$p$ of frames. \textbf{Spatial dropout:} zero $k$ rectangular regions of size $(f_h,f_w)$ across
all channels/frames. Severity schedules are in Table~\ref{tab:corr-severity}.

\subsection{Why membrane statistics detect corruption: a leaky-integrator theory}
\label{sec:theory}

We show that the detectability of each corruption is a closed-form consequence of the PLIF
state equation, not an empirical accident. The membrane is a leaky exponential accumulator of
the input current; each corruption perturbs that current in a structurally distinct way, and
the perturbation propagates to a predictable change in the moments of $\varphi$.

\paragraph{State equation and closed form.}
SpikingJelly's \texttt{MultiStepParametricLIFNode}~\citep{fang2021plif} updates, per channel,
$V[t]=(1-\tfrac1\tau)V[t-1]+W X[t]$, emitting a spike with reset when $V$ crosses threshold
$\theta$. Writing $\lambda\equiv1-1/\tau\in(0,1)$ and unrolling from $V[-1]=0$,
%
\begin{equation}
  V[t]=\sum_{k=0}^{t}\lambda^{k}\,W\,X[t-k],
  \label{eq:unroll}
\end{equation}
%
so $V$ is a causal, leaky linear filter of the event stream with memory horizon $\sim\tau$.
Two consequences: $V$ carries the temporal structure of recent input (so temporally-structured
corruptions leave a signature even when the marginal input is unchanged), and because $X\!\to\!V$
is linear, the moments of $V$ inherit closed-form dependence on the moments of $X$.

\paragraph{Steady-state moments.}
For wide-sense-stationary input with mean $m$, variance $s^2$, autocovariance $\gamma(j)$,
%
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[V]=\frac{Wm}{1-\lambda}=\tau Wm,\qquad
  \mathrm{Var}[V]=\frac{(Ws)^2}{1-\lambda^2}+2W^2\!\sum_{j\ge1}\gamma(j)\frac{\lambda^{j}}{1-\lambda^2}.
  \label{eq:moments}
\end{equation}
%
The mean tracks the input rate amplified by $\tau$; the variance tracks the input variance plus
an autocovariance term that depends on the \emph{time ordering}, not the marginal. These are the
levers each corruption pulls.

\paragraph{Per-corruption predictions.}
\textbf{\texttt{hot\_pixel}} adds a constant $\delta$, shifting $\mathbb{E}[V]$ by $\tau W\delta$
until $V$ saturates at $\theta$: affected $\mu_c$ separate from the clean range, giving
\emph{inevitable} detection (AUROC $\approx1$, monotone). \textbf{\texttt{temporal\_jitter}}
is a measure-preserving permutation: it leaves $m,s^2$ (hence $\mathbb{E}[V]$ and the first
variance term) unchanged but perturbs $\gamma(j)$ and the lag-1 autocorrelation of $V(t)$. The
deployed MDD uses \emph{only} the static moments $\varphi=[\mu,\sigma^2,\kappa]$ (lag-1
autocorrelation is named here as the \emph{mechanism}, not a feature we compute); jitter surfaces
in $\varphi$ because the deeper layers' larger effective $\tau$ convert this timing change into a
$\sigma^2$ shift -- which is why the layer-4 Mahalanobis branch, not any temporal statistic, is the
strongest single view. \textbf{\texttt{event\_rate\_shift}} scales $m\!\to\!\alpha m$,
displacing every channel's mean along one global-activity direction -- detectable by a 1-D
projection, with a phase transition once $\alpha$ leaves the clean band.
\textbf{\texttt{spatial\_dropout}} lowers the spatial input variance, hence $\sigma_c^2$, while
$\mu_c$ stays intact; but GAP pools away the spatial dimension before the moments are taken, so
the surviving signal makes the membrane look \emph{quieter and more typical} -- under one-sided
distance scoring the detector \emph{inverts} (AUROC $<0.5$). \textbf{\texttt{event\_flood}}
raises $m$ nearly uniformly, the same signature a genuinely busy clean scene produces, giving
near-chance per frame; what distinguishes it (spatially unstructured activity; a temporally
consistent bias) lives in the spatial dispersion GAP removes and in sequence-level consistency.
\textbf{\texttt{polarity\_flip}}: the trained detector is approximately polarity-symmetric, so
$WX$ -- and thus $\varphi$ -- is near-invariant; the AUROC ceiling is $\approx0.5$ by
construction.

\paragraph{Taxonomy.}
This yields a falsifiable taxonomy that organizes the whole results section:
\emph{magnitude} corruptions (hot\_pixel, event\_rate\_shift, jitter-via-variance) move
$\varphi$ \emph{away} from clean and are caught by distance detectors; \emph{structure}
corruptions (spatial\_dropout, the spatial part of event\_flood) leave the marginal moments
intact and require the spatial-dispersion features GAP discards or a rule that flags ``too
typical''; \emph{invariant} corruptions (polarity\_flip) are near-orthogonal to the membrane and
are an input-side problem. Every prediction follows from \eqref{eq:unroll}--\eqref{eq:moments}
with no parameters fit to corruption labels.

\subsection{Severity monotonicity: three guarantees and the spike--membrane gap}
\label{sec:theory-monotone}
The predictions above are qualitative. We now make them \emph{quantitative}: under mild assumptions
the membrane statistics, and the OOD score built on them, move \emph{monotonically} with corruption
severity. Let severity be $\gamma\ge0$ and model a corruption family as an additive drift on the
per-layer input current, $X_t(\gamma)=X_t(0)+\gamma\Delta_t+\epsilon_t(\gamma)$, with
$\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_t]=0$. Writing the membrane deviation $\delta V_t(\gamma)=V_t(\gamma)-V_t(0)$ and
linearising the PLIF update $V[t]=(1-\tfrac1\tau)V[t-1]+WX[t]$ in the sub-threshold regime
(bounded reset mismatch) gives
$\delta V_t(\gamma)=\lambda\,\delta V_{t-1}(\gamma)+W(\gamma\Delta_t+\epsilon_t)$, which unrolls to
$\delta V_t(\gamma)=\sum_{k=0}^{t}\lambda^{\,t-k}W(\gamma\Delta_k+\epsilon_k)$.

\begin{theorem}[Monotone membrane drift]
\label{thm:drift}
If the corruption induces a non-zero current drift $\mathbb{E}[\Delta_k]\neq0$ in at least one
monitored PLIF layer and the reset mismatch is bounded, then the temporal mean of the membrane
trajectory shifts at least linearly in $\gamma$,
$\mathbb{E}[\delta V_t(\gamma)]=\gamma\sum_{k=0}^{t}\lambda^{\,t-k}W\,\mathbb{E}[\Delta_k]$,
up to a bounded reset term. Hence the membrane mean coordinate $\mu$ of $\varphi$ is
OOD-informative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Monotone membrane variance]
\label{thm:var}
Suppose the corruption inflates the input-current covariance, $\mathrm{Cov}(X_t(\gamma))=
\Sigma_0+\gamma\Sigma_\Delta$ with $\Sigma_\Delta\succeq0$. Then for the stable linearised PLIF
recurrence the stationary membrane covariance is
$\Sigma_V(\gamma)=\big(\Sigma_0+\gamma\Sigma_\Delta\big)\,W^2/(1-\lambda^2)$, so
$\frac{d}{d\gamma}\,\mathrm{tr}\,\Sigma_V(\gamma)=\frac{W^2\,\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma_\Delta)}{1-\lambda^2}\ge0$.
Any corruption that increases the (PSD) input covariance therefore monotonically increases membrane
variance --- justifying the $\sigma^2$ and $\kappa$ coordinates of $\varphi$, not only the mean.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[MP-OOD score monotonicity]
\label{thm:score}
Let $\mathbb{E}[\varphi_\gamma]=m_0+\gamma b$ and $\mathrm{Cov}(\varphi_\gamma)=C_0+\gamma C_\Delta$
with $C_\Delta\succeq0$, where $(m_0,C_0)$ is the clean calibration model. The expected Mahalanobis
OOD score obeys
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[D^2(\varphi_\gamma)\right]=d+\gamma\,\mathrm{tr}(C_0^{-1}C_\Delta)+\gamma^2\,b^\top C_0^{-1}b,
  \label{eq:score-mono}
\end{equation}
which is non-decreasing in $\gamma$ and strictly increasing whenever the feature mean shifts
($b\neq0$) or its covariance grows ($C_\Delta\neq0$).
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:score} is the formal basis of the severity-monotonicity we measure (Spearman
$\rho=+1$ for hot\_pixel, event\_rate\_shift, event\_flood; Appendix~\ref{app:severity}). The lone
exception, \texttt{spatial\_dropout} ($\rho=-1$), is \emph{predicted}: GAP removes the dimension in
which the shift $b$ lives, so the score sees only a contraction toward $m_0$ and the sign of
\eqref{eq:score-mono} flips --- the inversion of Section~\ref{sec:theory} is exactly the failure of
the theorem's premise when the discriminative coordinate is pooled away.

\begin{proposition}[Spike counts discard sub-threshold OOD shift]
\label{prop:spike}
Consider a neuron with threshold $\theta$ whose in-distribution membrane lies in $[0,\theta-\varepsilon]$,
and a corruption that shifts every membrane value by $0<\delta<\varepsilon$. Then the spike train is
identically zero under both distributions ($s_t^{\mathrm{ID}}=s_t^{\mathrm{OOD}}=0$ for all $t$), so a
spike-count detector cannot separate them; yet the membrane means differ by exactly
$\bar V^{\mathrm{OOD}}-\bar V^{\mathrm{ID}}=\delta$.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:spike} is the conceptual novelty over prior spike-count SNN-OOD
\citep{martinez2023snnood}: \emph{spike-count OOD detects only threshold-crossing distribution
shift, whereas membrane-potential OOD detects both threshold-crossing and sub-threshold shift}, and
so strictly subsumes it. The sub-threshold potential is the richer signal precisely because spiking
is a lossy thresholded projection of it.

\subsection{The neuromorphic hardware advantage}
\label{sec:hardware}

We are careful to separate two cost claims. The \textbf{signal} $\varphi$ is zero added
\emph{inference} compute: on Loihi~\citep{davies2018loihi}, BrainScaleS~\citep{schemmel2010brainscales},
and SpiNNaker~\citep{furber2014spinnaker} the membrane potential $V$ is physical state the substrate
\emph{already maintains}, so producing $\varphi$ is an $O(\text{channels})$ register read plus three
moments per channel -- \emph{zero MACs, zero weight fetches, no second network}. The
\textbf{detector} (MDD) is \emph{not} claimed to be free: it operates on the $2112$-D vector
$\varphi$ off the critical path (a host or co-processor), with PCA projection, a Mahalanobis form,
and a cosine-$k$NN for RCF. Crucially, that cost is bounded by the size of $\varphi$ -- a few
thousand floats per frame -- and involves \emph{no second forward pass over the input}, the very
thing every ANN OOD detector requires (Table~\ref{tab:hardware}). So the comparison is not
``$\varphi$ beats ANN baselines on a GPU'' but ``$\varphi$ supplies an OOD signal within the SNN
inference budget, with a lightweight downstream test, whereas the ANN detectors need a full
non-spiking forward pass the deployment never runs.''

We do not claim $\varphi$ is the \emph{only} on-chip-feasible OOD signal: simple spike-rate or
spike-entropy statistics are equally cheap to read. We claim it is the \emph{richest} such signal
-- it provably subsumes spike counts (Proposition~\ref{prop:spike}) -- and the only one that, unlike
the ANN families below, needs no logits, gradients, or re-forwarding. We measure $\varphi$ in
simulation (SpikingJelly on GPU); the inference-cost claim is \emph{architectural} (it follows from
$V$ being a maintained register), and an on-silicon energy/latency measurement of the full pipeline
is future work.

\subsection{Compute accounting}
\label{sec:compute}
We separate the cost of the \emph{signal} from that of the \emph{detector}. The signal $\varphi$ is
extracted by Global Average Pooling the membrane state and taking three per-channel moments
$[\mu,\sigma^2,\kappa]$ over $704$ channels $\times$ $T{=}10$ steps (\texttt{monitor.py}): only local
reductions over state the forward pass already produced --- \emph{no learned transform, no weight
fetches, and no synaptic MACs}. This is the precise sense of ``zero added inference compute,'' and
it is a property of the extraction code, not an estimate. The detector (MDD) runs host-side on the
per-frame $2112$-D vector: standardize, a PCA-$64$ projection, two Mahalanobis branches (PCA-$64$
and the layer-$4$ block), and the cosine-$k$NN that conditions RCF against the clean reference (the
dominant term, and the only one that grows with the reference size; here a full $204$k-frame clean
reference). We report its end-to-end throughput as a \emph{measured} quantity:
$\sim\!2.7\times10^4$ frames/s ($\approx 0.037$\,ms/frame, batched scoring of held-out $\varphi$
through a fitted MDD, including all four branches and the full cosine-$k$NN) on a single NVIDIA
RTX~4060 laptop GPU. Reproducing the benchmark:
the one-time GPU cost is extraction --- $31$ runs $\times$ $\sim\!343$k frames at $B{=}1$, producing
$\sim\!90$--$150$\,GB of $\varphi$; every downstream stage then runs on the $2112$-D $\varphi$ in
minutes to an hour on one GPU.

\subsection{The Manifold-Decomposition Detector (MDD)}
\label{sec:mdd}

The theory classifies corruptions by how they move $\varphi$ relative to the clean manifold:
\emph{dilations} push outward (hot\_pixel, event\_rate\_shift; jitter in the deep-layer
subspace), \emph{contractions} pull toward the mode (spatial\_dropout, event\_flood) and thereby
\emph{invert} one-sided distance/density detectors, and \emph{invariant} corruptions
(polarity\_flip) leave no membrane signature. No single signed scalar can face outward, inward,
and into a subspace at once -- which is why every prior single detector (Mahalanobis, $k$-NN,
GMM, flow, learned fusion) topped out near $0.70$ macro and inverted on contractions. MDD stops
collapsing $\varphi$ to one number and scores three (optionally four) orthogonal, two-sided axes,
fused by a calibrated OR:
%
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{B1 radius} (two-sided isotropic energy): $|\,\lVert z\rVert-\mathbb{E}\lVert
        z\rVert|/\mathrm{sd}$ in a PCA-64 space -- the dilation/contraction energy axis.
  \item \textbf{B2 RCF} (Radial-Conditional, the novel core): a \emph{direction-conditioned}
        radius $|\,\lVert z\rVert-\mathbb{E}[\lVert z\rVert\mid\text{dir}]|/\mathrm{sd}$ via
        cosine-$k$NN; flags \emph{under}-dispersion -- the first branch to take contractions
        above chance per frame.
  \item \textbf{B3 L4 Mahalanobis} (one-sided) on the layer-4 $[\mu,\sigma^2,\kappa]$ block --
        the subspace axis for temporal\_jitter.
  \item \textbf{B4 spatial} (when \texttt{phi\_spatial} is extracted): Ledoit--Wolf Mahalanobis
        on the spatial-dispersion features.
\end{itemize}
%
Each branch is standardized on held-out clean and fused by a calibrated max. The shipped default
folds an empirical-covariance Mahalanobis into B1 (\texttt{radius}$'$) and replaces the plain max
with a studentized combiner $\max-\alpha\cdot\mathrm{median}$ ($\alpha\!\approx\!0.5$); the
derivation and ablation are in Appendices~\ref{app:mdd-branches}--\ref{app:fusion}. The detector
is fit once on clean-train only and is corruption-blind; an optional per-recording aggregation
integrates the consistent per-sequence bias. Figure~\ref{fig:mdd-flow} shows the full pipeline.

\subsection{Main result: MDD OOD detection}
\label{sec:main}
Tables~\ref{tab:mdd-frame}--\ref{tab:mdd-seq} report fused MDD AUROC per corruption/severity,
per-frame and per-sequence (source: \texttt{outputs/results/final\_results.csv}). One unsupervised,
corruption-blind score is already strong \emph{per frame} on the detectable corruptions at
severity~5 (\texttt{hot\_pixel} $1.00$, \texttt{temporal\_jitter} $0.85$, \texttt{event\_rate\_shift}
$0.86$); a short, bounded-latency $64$-frame window (Section~\ref{sec:window}) additionally lifts
\texttt{event\_flood} to $0.89$, so \emph{four of six} corruptions reach $\geq0.85$ without
whole-recording pooling. Full-sequence aggregation (Table~\ref{tab:mdd-seq}) sharpens the two
easiest to $1.00$, but it decides once per recording and we treat it as an optimistic upper bound,
not the headline. The two residuals are theory-predicted dead ends.

\paragraph{What aggregation does (and does not) buy.}
Because each Gen1-C corruption is applied to the \emph{whole} sequence, every frame of a corrupted
recording carries the \emph{same} bias; averaging the per-frame score over a recording integrates
that consistent bias and separates the classes at a rate $\sim\!\sqrt{\#\text{frames}}$. This is
why a near-chance per-frame signal (\texttt{event\_flood}, $0.551$) becomes \emph{perfectly}
separable per sequence ($1.000$): the lift is real but partly a property of the \emph{evaluation
protocol}, not evidence that any single frame is detectable. It would shrink if only a fraction of
frames were corrupted, or if the decision had to be taken per frame. We therefore report per-frame
AUROC as the conservative signal and treat per-sequence AUROC as an \emph{optimistic} bound at a
coarser (per-recording) decision granularity; the window sweep of Section~\ref{sec:window} makes
this dependence explicit, separating corruptions genuinely solved per frame from those solved only
by pooling.

\subsection{Windowed aggregation: the deployable operating point}
\label{sec:window}

setting is a \emph{short, fixed window} of $W$ frames, which bounds decision latency. Table~\ref{tab:window}
sweeps $W$ at severity~5 (source: \texttt{outputs/results/mdd\_window\_sweep.csv}). A modest window

\subsection{Discussion: physical causes and biological adaptation}
\label{sec:discussion}
The value of the closed-form theory is that each \emph{physical} axis (Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms})
maps to a distinct \emph{statistical} axis of the membrane moments, so a detector's behaviour on a
corruption is predictable from the sensor failure it models. One mechanism is worth dwelling on.
\texttt{event\_flood} -- one of the corruptions our membrane detector handles worst per frame
(near chance, Table~\ref{tab:mdd-frame}) -- inflates every channel's mean proportionally, mimicking
a busy scene (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).
\advisor{point 4 -- neuroscience of spike floods / adaptation.}
Biological neurons do not integrate a flood passively: \textbf{spike-frequency adaptation}
(calcium- and slow-K$^+$-driven)~\citep{benda2003sfa,gabbiani2014sfa} and \textbf{short-term
synaptic depression} (a divisive gain control)~\citep{abbott1997depression,rothman2009gaincontrol}
normalise sustained drive so the neuron responds to \emph{changes}, not absolute level. The PLIF
neuron has none of this -- a single static leak $\tau$, a linear accumulator
(Eq.~\ref{eq:unroll}), no adaptation -- so a flood simply scales the accumulator and looks like a
busy scene. The corruption biology handles best is the one our biologically-\emph{simplified}
model handles worst; equipping the front-end with adaptive (SFA / AdLIF) neurons is a concrete,
biologically-grounded path to both detecting \texttt{event\_flood} and restoring mAP
(future work, cf.\ Section~\ref{sec:motivation}).

\section{Limitations and the Open Challenge}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Honest residuals.}
Two corruptions remain unsolved from the membrane alone, and the theory predicts why.
\texttt{spatial\_dropout}'s signature is \emph{spatial} and GAP discards spatial layout; the
released spatial-dispersion features (\texttt{phi\_spatial}) feed MDD's spatial branch, which we
validated end-to-end on a controlled contraction (a signal present only in \texttt{phi\_spatial}
is lifted from fused-without-spatial AUROC $\approx0.50$ to $\approx1.00$;
Appendix~\ref{app:spatial-check}). \texttt{polarity\_flip} is a property of the trained network's
polarity invariance, not of our representation, and is undetectable from $V_{\mathrm{mem}}$ by
construction. We frame both as an \textbf{open challenge}: the Achilles' heel of event-based
perception, where corruptions are at once \emph{harmful} (Section~\ref{sec:motivation}) and
\emph{hard} to detect cheaply.

\paragraph{Other limitations.}
(i)~The zero-cost claim is \emph{architectural} and applies to the \emph{signal} only: $\varphi$ is
free at inference, but the MDD detector (PCA/Mahalanobis/cosine-$k$NN) runs host-side on the
$2112$-D vector; we measure in simulation (SpikingJelly on GPU) and an on-silicon energy/latency
measurement of the full pipeline is future work. (i$'$)~$\varphi$ is read from the reset-inclusive
membrane register, so the linear sub-threshold theory is an approximation; a strictly
spike-excluded $V$ and a reset-aware analysis are untested.
(ii)~AUROC are single-split point estimates without bootstrap CIs, and we do not yet report
sensitivity to the PCA dimension, the cosine-$k$NN $k$, or the clean-calibration sample size.
(iii)~Single dataset
(Gen1); DSEC/Gen4 transfer is untested. (iv)~Severities are synthetic monotone transforms, not
measured sensor failures -- the contribution is the benchmark/protocol/feature release.
(v)~Per-sequence AUROC assumes a corruption is consistent across a whole recording (as in Gen1-C);
the strong per-sequence numbers (e.g.\ \texttt{event\_flood} $1.000$) are partly a consequence of
that protocol and of the coarser decision granularity, and should not be read as per-frame
detectability. (vi)~AUROC is an oracle-labelled \emph{separability} metric, not a value the
corruption-blind detector reproduces at a fixed operating threshold in deployment.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced OOD detection for event-camera spiking detectors from the \emph{free} sub-threshold
membrane potential, the Gen1-C corruption benchmark with its physical-mechanism grounding, a
closed-form theory of per-corruption detectability, and the Manifold-Decomposition Detector that
follows from a dilation/contraction diagnosis. A pretrained SOTA detector collapses under these
corruptions; $\varphi$ is a zero-added-inference-cost OOD signal that needs no second forward pass
and provably subsumes spike-rate signals; and one unsupervised score takes four of six corruptions
to $\geq0.85$ with a bounded $64$-frame window (whole-recording pooling only sharpens the two
easiest). The two residuals are theory-predicted and released as an open challenge, with a
concrete, biologically-grounded path -- spatial-dispersion features and adaptive neurons --
toward closing them.

\label{app:repr}

\section{Temporal Representations (Explored, Not Used by MDD)}
\label{app:temporal}
For completeness we also explored temporal representations of the membrane trace -- handcrafted
per-frame statistics of $V(t)$ over the $T{=}10$ SNN steps (lag-1 autocorrelation, CUSUM,
high-frequency energy), a 1D-CNN autoencoder on the pooled trajectory, and margin histograms
($V-\theta$). Under the same leakage-safe split they give only a modest gain over static $\varphi$
on the hard corruptions ($\sim0.05$--$0.08$ AUROC on
\texttt{event\_flood}/\texttt{spatial\_dropout}), and are \emph{not} part of the deployed MDD, which
uses only the static moments $\varphi=[\mu,\sigma^2,\kappa]$. We report them as explored-but-unused.

\section{MDD Hyperparameter Sensitivity}
\label{app:sensitivity}
To test robustness to its three free knobs, \texttt{analysis/mdd\_sensitivity.py} sweeps one at a
time around the shipped defaults --- PCA dimensionality \texttt{k\_pca} ($64$), cosine-$k$NN
neighbours \texttt{k\_nn} ($64$) for the RCF branch, and clean-reference size \texttt{n\_ref}
($15000$) --- refitting the MDD for each value and re-scoring the existing $\varphi$ (no
re-extraction), reporting fused AUROC at $W{=}1$ and the deployable $W{=}64$ window. Run on the full
L5 grid (\texttt{outputs/results/mdd\_sensitivity.csv}, $168$ rows), \textbf{MDD is insensitive to
all three knobs}: at $W{=}64$ the fused AUROC of every corruption varies by $\le 0.01$ across
\texttt{k\_pca}$\in[16,256]$ and by $\le 0.024$ across \texttt{k\_nn}$\in[8,128]$ (the largest
swings are on the two residuals --- \texttt{spatial\_dropout} $0.533\!\to\!0.557$,
\texttt{polarity\_flip} $0.572\!\to\!0.584$ --- while the four solved corruptions move $\le 0.01$),
and it is flat in \texttt{n\_ref} (the RCF uses the full clean reference regardless). The shipped
defaults sit on this plateau, so no per-dataset tuning is required (Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity}).

\section{Free-Rider Validity Check}
\label{app:freerider}
\texttt{free\_rider\_ablation.py} compares OOD AUROC from (A) trained-SNN membrane, (B)
random-init SNN membrane, (C) raw-input statistics; the premise of the whole paper requires
(A)$\gg$(C) (otherwise $\varphi$ is just a re-encoding of the input). A pilot (5 sequences,
Mahalanobis, L5) gives, on \texttt{hot\_pixel}: (A)~$\mathbf{0.998} >$ (B)~$0.822 >$ (C)~$0.716$ ---
$+0.18$ from \emph{learning} (A$-$B) and $+0.28$ from SNN processing over raw input statistics
(A$-$C). On \texttt{event\_flood} all three cluster near chance ($0.50$--$0.56$), consistent with
its theoretical indistinguishability. The make-or-break premise holds in the pilot. \emph{These are
provisional (a biased first-$N$ 5-sequence subset); the full-grid run is the remaining gating
experiment.}

\section{Spatial-Branch Mechanism Check}
\label{app:spatial-check}
On a controlled contraction whose signal lives \emph{only} in \texttt{phi\_spatial} (GAP'd
$\varphi$ matched to clean): fused-without-spatial AUROC $=0.496\to$ fused-with-spatial $=1.000$;
spatial branch alone $=1.000$ -- confirming the wiring. On the banked extraction the spatial
branch \emph{nails} \texttt{event\_flood} ($1.000$) but stays below chance on
\texttt{spatial\_dropout} ($0.411$), consistent with the contraction ceiling.
