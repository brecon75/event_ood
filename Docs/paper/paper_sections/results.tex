\section{The Manifold-Decomposition Detector and OOD Results}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{From diagnosis to architecture.}
The theory of Section~\ref{sec:theory} classifies corruptions by how they move $\varphi$
relative to the clean manifold: \emph{dilations} push it outward (\texttt{hot\_pixel},
\texttt{event\_rate\_shift}; \texttt{temporal\_jitter} in the deep-layer subspace),
\emph{contractions} pull it toward the mode (\texttt{spatial\_dropout}, \texttt{event\_flood})
and thereby \emph{invert} one-sided distance/density detectors, and \emph{invariant}
corruptions (\texttt{polarity\_flip}) leave no membrane signature. No single signed scalar can
face outward, inward, and into a subspace at once -- which is why every prior single detector
(Mahalanobis, $k$-NN, GMM, flow, learned fusion) topped out near $0.70$ macro and inverted on
contractions. The Manifold-Decomposition Detector (MDD) stops collapsing $\varphi$ to one
number: it scores three (optionally four) orthogonal, two-sided axes and fuses them with a
calibrated max -- a genuine, unsupervised OR. The branches are the global radius
$|\,\lVert z\rVert-\mathbb{E}\lVert z\rVert|$, the direction-conditioned radial score (RCF;
the novel core, which flags \emph{under}-dispersion and so detects contractions), a
deep-layer (L4) Mahalanobis for timing, and -- when the spatial-dispersion features are
extracted -- a spatial branch on the structure global pooling discards. Full derivation in the
supplementary \texttt{novel.md}.

\paragraph{Headline.}
One unsupervised, corruption-blind score takes four of six corruptions to $\geq 0.96$ with
sequence aggregation. The two residuals are not noise but \emph{predicted} dead ends:
\texttt{spatial\_dropout}'s signature is spatial and global pooling discarded it (the
released \texttt{phi\_spatial} branch is the prescribed fix -- Section~\ref{sec:limitations}),
and \texttt{polarity\_flip} is a property of the trained network's invariance, not of our
representation.
